## 2. Option B on Marcus's criterion — "scientifically reasonable (good) vs more complexity (bad)"
**What (B) is, at its smallest.** Not a third column. The model already has a Southern Ocean pair (HS_SH / HD_SH); what it lacks is a ONE-PASS window: interior (CDW, layer k ≈ 8–9) → HD_SH → HS_SH → back into the interior at depth (AAIW / AABW, layer k′), ~30 Sv, one-way — with the SH cold box thin enough that the pass is short. Today that window is a two-way diffusive exchange (`tau_q` 538 yr, 38 Sv; `u_hs`, 18 Sv) whose partner box (HD_SH) equilibrates with the air. The loop's SH branch (legs 5–6, built, tested) is 60 % of this; the missing 40 % is ONE leg (HS_SH → SH interior layer k′ instead of → ML_SH), a per-hemisphere `h_hs`, and the SH-only switch. Half a day plus a probe on the existing harness. The full 3-column model (own atmosphere coupling, own pump field, every registered mode refit, the app's box count) is weeks and is NOT what the science needs.
**Scientifically reasonable — the case FOR.** (i) It is the single largest feature of the real carbon cycle the two-column model cannot express: the Southern Ocean is where ~30 Sv of old deep water meets the atmosphere and leaves again within years (the tree's own citation chain: Talley 2013 AABW 29 Sv, `data-raw/build_u_hs_prior.R`); every ESM and every intermediate-complexity model that resolves latitude has it. (ii) It targets an OBSERVATION the model misses by more than anything else in the scorecard: the SH cold box's D +0.14 vs −1.06 (z +25 at 09-20 t), and with it `hs_surf_d13c`'s strain and the SO surface's age (P5's held-out MLO−SPO Δ¹⁴C moved toward obs under the loop, −5.9 → −3.8 vs −1.5). (iii) The first-principles pre-check closes: 30 Sv through a 1.1 × 10¹⁸ m³ interior with a half-rejuvenated pass gives a steady deep Δ¹⁴C ≈ −179 vs the −175 target; at 30 Sv the SH cold box's residence is 7.5 yr (2.65 × 10¹³ m² × 269 m) against a 12-yr isotopic e-folding — "half-rejuvenated" is what the geometry gives. (iv) It is PEDAGOGICAL in exactly CCX's sense ([[ccx_pedagogical_not_emulator]]): "the Southern Ocean is the deep ocean's window" is the one sentence about ocean carbon an undergraduate should leave with, and the app cannot say it today.
**Complexity — the case AGAINST.** (i) Three more structural constants (W_so, k_up, k_dn) and a hemispheric `h_hs`; the ocean's NH and SH code paths stop being mirror images. (ii) It is deep-Δ¹⁴C-exposed: the pre-check is a steady-state two-box estimate, and the 09-15 d / 09-20 v history says the profile rows bite at ~5 Sv for every limb so far — the SH-only, deep-return geometry has NOT been run, so "compatible" is a prediction, not a result. (iii) It does NOT buy gate D's asymmetry (NH-side) or the offset — anyone hoping it closes the δ¹³C gradient will be disappointed; §3 of the scoping note shows the SH side can be made right regionally while the asymmetry vanishes. (iv) The tree already carries nine inert switches from this arc (§4); (B) adds to that unless the arc's dead switches are pruned first.
**Verdict (mine, for Marcus to rule on).** By the criterion as stated, the MINIMAL (B) passes: it adds one physically real, observation-anchored, textbook mechanism for a measured 25-σ regional miss, at one leg's complexity — provided it is built as a PROBE with a preregistered deep-Δ¹⁴C bound (the same P6 rule: dead if the profile rows reject it below ~20 Sv) and provided the arc's refuted switches are pruned in the same breath so the net complexity is flat.
