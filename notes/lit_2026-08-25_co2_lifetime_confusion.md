## The confusion, stated once

Two different timescales are both correctly called "lifetime":

- **Residence / turnover time** — atmospheric carbon ÷ gross outflux. ~4–5 yr. Set by the *gross*
  exchange with ocean and biosphere, which is ~20× the net anthropogenic flux.
- **Adjustment time** — how long a *perturbation* to the stock decays. Centuries, with a long tail.
  Set by the *net* imbalance, which is where the buffer chemistry and the deep-ocean mixing enter.

The error is to measure the first and draw a conclusion about the second — typically: *"a CO₂
molecule only lasts 5 years, so the rise cannot be anthropogenic."* Both numbers are true; they
answer different questions, and our model emits both from one state.

## Peer-reviewed instances, each with its published rebuttal

| # | The claim | Vintage / venue | Rebuttal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Essenhigh** — the 5–15 yr residence time "establishes" the ~100-yr rise is **not** anthropogenic but a response to rising temperature | 2009, *Energy & Fuels* **23**(5) 2773–2784, doi `10.1021/ef800581r` | **Cawley 2011**, *Energy & Fuels*, doi `10.1021/ef200914u` — the rise depends on the **net difference** between fluxes, not their total volume, so it is essentially **independent of residence time** |
| 2 | **Harde** — an alternative single-equation accounting scheme giving only **4.3 %** of today's atmospheric CO₂ as anthropogenic | 2017, *Global and Planetary Change* **152** 19–26 (PII S0921818116304787) | **Köhler, Hauck, Völker, Wolf-Gladrow, Butzin, Halpern, Rice, Zeebe 2018**, *GPC* **164** 67–71 — *"the most simple model of the carbon cycle must at minimum contain equations of at least two reservoirs … A single equation is fundamentally at odds with basic theory and observations"*, and its item (i) is explicitly *"clarify the difference between CO₂ atmospheric residence time and adjustment time"* |
| 3 | **Stallinga** — *"the adjustment time is never larger than the residence time and can, thus, not be longer than about 5 years"* | **2023**, *Entropy* **25**(2) 384, doi `10.3390/e25020384` | **Burton**, comment (**preprint, not peer-reviewed** — sealevel.info): the relationship is **backwards**; adjustment time exceeds residence time precisely because some processes that shorten residence do not shorten adjustment |

**Case 3 is the most useful one for us and the most recent.** It is a 2023 paper, in a
Scopus-indexed journal, whose *title* is the exact distinction the app teaches — and it gets the
inequality the wrong way round. It was also immediately amplified as *"New Study: Atmospheric CO₂
Residence Time Is Only 5 Years – Too Short To 'Affect The Climate'"* (NoTricksZone, 2023-03-23),
which is the propagation path the tab is aimed at.

**Note the trajectory:** 2009 → 2017 → 2023. This is not a settled historical curiosity; it recurs
about every 6–8 years in a journal outside the carbon-cycle literature (a fuels journal, a
geoscience journal, an information-theory journal), each time without carbon-cycle review.

## The correct-side anchors, for the tab's citations

- **Archer et al. 2009**, *"Atmospheric Lifetime of Fossil Fuel Carbon Dioxide"*, Annu. Rev. Earth
  Planet. Sci. — the multi-model long tail; ~25 % lasting essentially forever.
- **Joos et al. 2013**, ACP **13** 2793–2825, doi `10.5194/acp-13-2793-2013` — already DESIGN gate
  12's benchmark, and already in the objective.
- **Cawley 2011** and **Köhler et al. 2018** above double as the clearest short explanations of the
  distinction in the peer-reviewed record.
