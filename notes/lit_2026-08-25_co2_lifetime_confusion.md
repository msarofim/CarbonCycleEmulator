# Published examples of the CO₂ "lifetime" confusion — sweep, 2026-08-25

**Why this file.** DESIGN §7 calls the app's "How long does it last?" tab *"the tab the project
exists for"*: one model producing **turnover ~4.7 yr** ("how long an average molecule stays") beside
**adjustment** ("how long the added amount stays" — ~26 % airborne at 100 yr). This is the
target audience's misconception, so the published record of people making it is the evidence that
the tab is needed. Compiled at Marcus's request; **vintages stated throughout**
([[weight_recent_literature]]).

## The confusion, stated once

Two different timescales are both correctly called "lifetime":

- **Residence / turnover time** — atmospheric carbon ÷ gross outflux. ~4–5 yr. Set by the *gross*
  exchange with ocean and biosphere, which is ~20× the net anthropogenic flux.
- **Adjustment time** — how long a *perturbation* to the stock decays. Centuries, with a long tail.
  Set by the *net* imbalance, which is where the buffer chemistry and the deep-ocean mixing enter.

The error is to measure the first and draw a conclusion about the second — typically: *"a CO₂
molecule only lasts 5 years, so the rise cannot be anthropogenic."* Both numbers are true; they
answer different questions, and our model emits both from one state.

## Peer-reviewed instances, each with its published rebuttal

| # | The claim | Vintage / venue | Rebuttal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Essenhigh** — the 5–15 yr residence time "establishes" the ~100-yr rise is **not** anthropogenic but a response to rising temperature | 2009, *Energy & Fuels* **23**(5) 2773–2784, doi `10.1021/ef800581r` | **Cawley 2011**, *Energy & Fuels*, doi `10.1021/ef200914u` — the rise depends on the **net difference** between fluxes, not their total volume, so it is essentially **independent of residence time** |

| 3 | **Stallinga** — *"the adjustment time is never larger than the residence time and can, thus, not be longer than about 5 years"* | **2023**, *Entropy* **25**(2) 384, doi `10.3390/e25020384` | **Burton**, comment (**preprint, not peer-reviewed** — sealevel.info): the relationship is **backwards**; adjustment time exceeds residence time precisely because some processes that shorten residence do not shorten adjustment |

**Case 3 is the most useful one for us and the most recent.** It is a 2023 paper, in a
Scopus-indexed journal, whose *title* is the exact distinction the app teaches — and it gets the
inequality the wrong way round. It was also immediately amplified as *"New Study: Atmospheric CO₂
Residence Time Is Only 5 Years – Too Short To 'Affect The Climate'"* (NoTricksZone, 2023-03-23),
which is the propagation path the tab is aimed at.

**Note the trajectory:** 2009 → 2017 → 2023. This is not a settled historical curiosity; it recurs
about every 6–8 years in a journal outside the carbon-cycle literature (a fuels journal, a
geoscience journal, an information-theory journal), each time without carbon-cycle review.

## ⇒ THE MODERN FORM OF THE ERROR IS NOT "5 YEARS". IT IS A SINGLE EXPONENTIAL.

Dengler & Reid (42-yr half-life ⇒ 60.6-yr e-fold) and Spencer (2.05 %/yr ⇒ 48.8-yr e-fold) both
use **one-box, single-time-constant removal**. Against Joos et al. 2013 Table 5 (the multi-model
mean already wired into DESIGN gate 12), fraction of a pulse still airborne:

| yr | Joos T5 | Spencer 2.05 %/yr | Dengler–Reid 42 yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.596 | 0.664 | 0.719 |
| 50 | 0.487 | 0.359 | 0.438 |
| 100 | 0.409 | 0.129 | 0.192 |
| **300** | **0.322** | **0.002** | **0.007** |
| **1000** | **0.235** | **0.000** | **0.000** |

Joos's `a0 = 0.2173` is an **asymptote**: ~22 % that never leaves. **A single exponential gives zero
at t → ∞ by construction**, so the disagreement is not a parameter choice — it is structural.

# Part 3 — Marcus's corrections, 2026-08-25

## 3.1 The history is part of the record: BOTH toyed with "mostly natural"

Part 2 reported Spencer's movement toward the mainstream and Curry's distance from Salby. That is
accurate but **incomplete, and the incompleteness flatters them**. Marcus's correction: *both*
entertained a mostly-natural origin for the modern rise, and for years.

- **Spencer, 2009-01-21:** had "usually accepted" the anthropogenic premise, but "an anthropogenic
  explanation … becomes less obvious". **2009-05-11:** *"20 % of long-term rise is anthropogenic,
  while the other 80 % is natural."* That is not a hedge; it is the mostly-natural position, stated
  as a model result. He reached ~85 %/15 % only by **2022** — a **thirteen-year** interval.
- **Curry** hosted the carbon-cycle-questions thread (2011), Salby's presentation (2016), and both
  2023 guest posts. She declines to endorse Salby's strongest claim, but the venue is the point:
  she platformed the mostly-natural argument repeatedly across **twelve years** without a post
  retiring it.

**Why this belongs in the file rather than in a footnote.** Two of the five DOE Climate Working
Group authors spent over a decade with an unresolved position on whether the CO₂ rise is
anthropogenic — a question the isotopes settled before either post was written. That is the
strongest available argument that the app's tab addresses a live confusion at the top of the
policy chain, not a blog-comment curiosity. **Report the arc, not the endpoint.**

## 3.2 Spencer's FIXED EQUILIBRIUM is a second defect, and we have measured its analogue

Part 2 named the single exponential. Marcus names the other half: Spencer's sink is
`k·(C − 295 ppm)` with **both `k` and the 295 held constant forever**. The ocean's uptake
efficiency never degrades.

That is not a separate curiosity — **it is the same assumption our own model carries a switch
for.** `p$revelle_frozen = 1` pins the buffer factor at its 1750 value so that, in the code's own
words, *"the ocean therefore keeps absorbing at its preindustrial efficiency."* And we measured it
this session (`calibration/diag_af_trend_levers.R`):

> **Freezing the buffer removes +0.0084/decade of airborne-fraction trend — 57 % of the entire
> observed +0.0148/decade.** It was the single largest working contributor to the model's AF
> time-dependence.

So the assumption Spencer makes is worth, in our model, **more than half of the observed
airborne-fraction trend**, and discarding it is not a detail of the tail — it is visible in the
1959–2024 record the model is fitted to.

**And it is where the practical payload sits.** With a fixed equilibrium and constant `k`, constant
emissions give a **finite** stable concentration `C∞ = 295 + E/k`. That is exactly the mechanism by
which Dengler & Reid reach *"CO₂ stabilises near 475 ppm and the Paris goals are fulfilled"* on
unchanged emissions. The projection is the point of the error, not a by-product of it.

## 3.3 Our model is not competing on equal terms, and 12a should not be read as if it were

Part 2 flagged that our model fails gate 12a at TH 20/50/100 where Spencer's one-box model passes,
and called that "uncomfortable". **Marcus's correction: it is not necessarily a defect at all.**

Spencer's model has to match **one** series — total atmospheric concentration — with a sink
coefficient free to absorb whatever that requires. Ours must simultaneously satisfy CO₂ at two
stations, δ¹³C at two stations, Δ¹⁴C in both hemispheres including the bomb transient, the ice-core
record, the interhemispheric gradients in two species, the deep-ocean Δ¹⁴C, the GCB sinks, and the
seasonal cycles — **21 likelihood streams**, of which the isotopes are the ones that pin the *gross*
fluxes and therefore the partition a one-box model simply asserts.

A model tuned to one curve **should** fit that curve better. The question is what it costs
elsewhere, and gate 12b is the answer: **structure right, century-scale magnitude low.** Stated as a
comparison rather than an indictment:

| | streams fitted | 12a (TH 20/50/100) | 12b (tail ratio) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spencer 2023 | 1 (concentration) | passes | **fails by 36×** |
| Dengler & Reid 2023 | 1 (concentration) | passes | **fails by 16×** |
| **this model** | **21, incl. δ¹³C and Δ¹⁴C** | 1–4 % low | **passes, 9.4× above its own null** |

## 4.1 CORRECTION: three independent lines, and the isotopes are the THIRD

Parts 1–2 leaned on the isotopes as what settles attribution. **That is not the argument, and
overstating the isotopes' role understates how thoroughly settled this is.** Marcus's framing:

1. **Ice cores.** 800 kyr at 180–300 ppm (Jansen et al. 2007); today's value is far outside the
   range of the past million years.
2. **Mass balance.** Cumulative emissions exceed the atmospheric increase by more than 2×, so the
   natural world is a **net sink**. Needs no model, no isotopes, no fitted parameter.
3. **Isotopes + carbon-cycle modelling**, "fully understood by the 1980s".

**Lines 1 and 2 each suffice on their own.** The draft says so directly: those two facts *"are
enough in and of themselves to be strong evidence for an anthropogenic cause"*.

**Checked against GCB 2025 — our own scored dataset — and it holds with room to spare:**

| window | emitted | atmosphere gained | ratio | nature's net uptake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1851–2010 (the draft's window) | **591.0 GtC** | 217.3 GtC (102.3 ppm) | **2.72** | 373.7 GtC |
| 1850–2024 (full) | 751.0 | 291.6 (137.3 ppm) | **2.58** | 459.4 |
| 1959–2024 (post-seam only) | 537.3 | 231.7 (109.1 ppm) | **2.32** | 305.6 |

The draft's 2011 figures (">470 GtC", "about 200 GtC", ratio > 2) are reproduced and **strengthened**
— today's LUC estimates are larger, so the ratio is higher.

**Keep two questions apart, because our model sits on the second.** Mass balance settles
**attribution** (*is the rise anthropogenic?*). Isotopes constrain **structure** — the gross fluxes,
the partition, and hence the adjustment time. Our 21 streams are not needed to answer question one;
they are what makes question two answerable.

## 4.2 The timeline is 16 years longer than Part 1 said — and the physics is 68 years old

| vintage | what |
|---|---|
| **1957** | **Revelle & Suess**, *Tellus* IX — already distinguish the two: the *"exchange rate of isotopically labeled CO₂ … is not identical with the rate of absorption"*, attributed to *"the peculiar buffer mechanism of sea water"*. They also **considered and rejected** ocean warming as the cause |
| 1975 | **Oeschger et al.**, *Tellus* 27:168–192 — box-diffusion model; carbon-cycle models stopped being stirred reactors fifty years ago |
| **1979** | **Rodhe & Björkström**, *Tellus* 31:269–278 — shows **analytically** that residence and adjustment times decouple when flux is not proportional to reservoir content, and that adjustment can be several times turnover |
| **1993** | **Starr**, *Energy* 18:1297–1310 — **the first published instance**, on ¹⁴C, with Essenhigh's logic, **sixteen years earlier** |
| **1997** | **O'Neill et al.**, *Climatic Change* 37:491–503, *"Measuring Time in the Greenhouse"* — **written specifically to clear up this confusion** |
| 2009 / 2017 / 2023 | Essenhigh / Harde / Stallinga (Part 1) |

**Part 1's "recurs every 6–8 years since 2009" is superseded.** The correct statement: **the physics
was settled in 1957 and formalised in 1979; the confusion first reached print in 1993; a paper was
written in 1997 for the sole purpose of ending it; and it has recurred at least four times since.**

## 4.4 The isotope-separation reductio — a candidate app panel

Essenhigh's model requires ¹²CO₂ to have a residence time **a factor of 3 shorter than ¹⁴CO₂**.
Real fractionation: **~0.2 %** for the physical air-sea kinetic effect, **2–3 %** for biological
uptake. So the model needs an isotopic separation **~100× larger than the largest real mechanism**,
between species that differ in mass by under 5 %.

**Our model is unusually well placed to show this**, because it carries α values for exactly those
processes (`alpha_as_down`, `alpha_as_up`, `alpha_photo`) and runs the ¹²C/¹³C/¹⁴C pulses from one
state. Recorded as a candidate panel, not built.

## 4.5 Numbers from the draft worth keeping

- **Solubility:** 6.9–10.2 ppm per °C of SST (Denman et al. 2007). Against the observed **137 ppm**
  rise that needs **13.5–19.9 °C** of SST warming; observed is ~1 °C. **The hypothesis fails by
  10–15×**, and Revelle & Suess rejected it in 1957.
- **Revelle factor 8–16** (Sabine et al. 2004): raising ocean carbon 1 % at equilibrium needs
  atmospheric carbon to rise 8–16 %. This is the quantitative form of §3.2's fixed-equilibrium
  defect.
- **Deforestation** was the dominant human source until the early 20th century and is ~15 % of
  fossil today — Essenhigh's Fig. 2 omitted it (CDIAC hosted it on the same page).
