## 2. What sets the cold boxes' isotopic state today (the HILDA pair, in numbers)
At the canonical (solved field, PI): ML δ¹³C 1.98 / 2.42 (NH / SH), **HS 2.75 / 2.97**, HD 0.69 / 0.99, bottom 0.69 / 0.99; Δ¹⁴C ML −40, HS −96, HD −139, bottom −191 (target −175). The cold box is HEAVIER than its own mixed layer by 0.6–0.8 ‰ — that is the cold-water equilibrium (ε_DIC−g grows as T falls), and gate D reads it as D ≈ 0, "equilibrated". Its water budget: HS ↔ HD at `u_hs` 21.8 m/yr over `h_hs` 269 m = **12-yr residence** (13.8 / 18.3 Sv NH / SH over `a_hs` 2.0 / 2.7 × 10¹³ m²; 26 GtC yr⁻¹ of carbon); HD ↔ interior at `tau_q` 538 yr (29 / 38 Sv, two-way); air-sea isotopic e-folding of the HS box ≈ h·(DIC/[CO₂*])/k_w ≈ 269 × 85 / 1912 ≈ **12 yr**. So the cold box is half-way between its deep source and the air — and its deep source (HD, 0.7 / 1.0 × 10¹⁷ m³) is a two-way exchange partner of both the surface and the interior, so it sits between them rather than delivering interior water through. The real Southern Ocean surface is un-equilibrated (D −1.06) because ~30 Sv of Circumpolar Deep Water (a 10¹⁸ m³ reservoir) reaches it and leaves again within a few years (Deacon cell; the residence is recollection — Marshall & Speer 2012-class, uncited in the tree).
**First-principles pre-check of a 30-Sv SO cell's deep bill** (the `[[quantitative_pre_check]]` discipline): ventilating the model's 1.1 × 10¹⁸ m³ interior at 30 Sv is a 1,200-yr turnover; with the cold box half-rejuvenated (−60 ‰, the pass being short) the steady deep Δ¹⁴C is (−60/1200 − 1000/8267)/(1/1200 + 1/8267) ≈ **−179 ‰ against the −175 target**. A SO cell of the observed size is compatible with the deep age IF the surface pass is short. The loop I built failed that condition twice: the NH sink took −30 ‰ ML water, and the SH return went through the ML (a 14-yr pass at 15 Sv over 269 m).

## 5. Hector's carbon cycle (Hartin et al. 2015 GMD 8:939; Hartin et al. 2016 BG 13:4329; `src/ocean_component.cpp` at JGCRI/hector main, read 09-21)
**Structure.** One well-mixed atmosphere; land = vegetation / detritus / soil per biome with NPP = NPP₀ (1 + β ln(C/C₀)), β 0.36, and RH ∝ Q10^(T/10), Q10 2.45 (GMD Table 2). Ocean = **four boxes, no diffusion, no biology, no isotopes**: high-latitude surface HL (> 55°, both hemispheres together, 15 % of area, 100 m, 5.4 × 10¹⁵ m³, 2 °C), low-latitude surface LL (85 %, 100 m, 3.06 × 10¹⁶ m³, 22 °C), intermediate IO (900 m, 3.24 × 10¹⁷ m³), deep DO (2677 m, 9.64 × 10¹⁷ m³) (BG Table 1). Transports (m³ s⁻¹, from the source's `make_connection` calls): the thermohaline loop **tt = 72 Sv**: LL → HL → DO → IO → LL; the high-latitude cell **tu = 49 Sv**: IO → HL → DO → IO; exchanges twi = 20.8 Sv LL ↔ IO and tid = 12.5 Sv IO ↔ DO (Lenton 2000 / Knox & McElroy 1984). Each flux = T × C_i / V_i (BG eq. 3). Air-sea per box F = k α ΔpCO₂ with Wanninkhof-1992 k at a single U₁₀ = 6.7 m s⁻¹ over both boxes; full carbonate chemistry (Zeebe & Wolf-Gladrow) per surface box; HL T = ΔT − 13, LL T = ΔT + 7. **Tuning target: "~100 Pg C yr⁻¹ from the surface high-latitude box to the deep ocean"** (GMD Table 2, BG §2) — a gross carbon transport, not an age or a profile.
**Residence times implied** (V / inflow, computed here): **HL 1.4 yr** (121 Sv into 5.4 × 10¹⁵ m³), LL ~10 yr, IO ~67 yr, **DO ~250 yr** (121 Sv into 9.6 × 10¹⁷ m³). Hector's deep ocean turns over in ~250 years — six times faster than the Δ¹⁴C age says — and nothing in Hector objects, because it carries no ¹⁴C, no interior profile and no δ¹³C; it is fitted to atmospheric CO₂ and a gross flux. The HL box is a 1.4-yr pass of cold water: a pure solubility pump (HL takes up, LL outgasses; GMD Fig. 2 caption).
**What transfers, and what does not.** (a) Hector's HL box is fed from the intermediate box (tu: IO → HL) AND from the surface (tt: LL → HL) — the "aged feed" of T2, and the deep return to the surface is via IO → LL, not through the cold box. In CCX the analogous interior → HS supply exists as two-way exchange (`tau_q` → HD at 29–38 Sv, `u_hs` → HS at 14–18 Sv per hemisphere); Hector's is 49 Sv one-way. (b) Hector treats the cold box as ONE global box — it cannot pose gate D's question at all; the NH/SH asymmetry is invisible to it by construction. (c) The single wind speed over both boxes is the same g_h = g_l assumption HILDA makes; NCEP says 3.3×. (d) The direct lesson is a caution, not a recipe: **a fast advective overturning "works" in Hector because no observable it fits can see the deep age**; CCX's objective can (the profile rows), which is exactly why the same loop dies here. (e) One structural idea worth keeping: Hector closes the cell through the INTERMEDIATE box (DO → IO → LL), not through the cold surface box — the SH return in my loop went HD → HS → ML, which is what made the pass long. (f) The "100 Pg C yr⁻¹" target itself: CCX's HS → HD gross carbon transport is 11 + 15 = **26 GtC yr⁻¹** (`u_hs` × a_hs × DIC, both hemispheres) — a quarter of Hector's, and constrained by the deep Δ¹⁴C rows rather than tuned; Hector's 100 is what a 121-Sv, 1.4-yr cold box carries.

## 6. Larger updates, ranked — what each buys, what each costs, what would be known before building
**(A) Register the observed hemispheric asymmetries as a SET and let a refit trade them against a hemisphere-resolved D row.** The smallest real update: promote gate D's four regional D's to likelihood rows (a σ from the bottles' bootstrap — a 27th–30th row; the "hemisphere-resolved disequilibrium row", option B of 09-20 s), and free, with cited centers, `k_w_fac_SH` (NCEP 1.14), `k_w_fac_hs` (NCEP 3.3), `pump_hs` per hemisphere (the HS prior's PAZ center 23 vs the fitted 79 — the NH subpolar half was the stated gap in that prior) and `h_hs` / `u_hs` per hemisphere. Known now: ×1.14 alone is a third of gate D at −102; the residual goes the other way, so the refit would have to find the NH-side mechanism (§3) or accept the residual. Cost: a refit (Torch, ~5 h) and the row's construction (a day). Risk: the wind asymmetry is right and still fails, because gate D's asymmetry lives in the NH mixed layer (+0.25 short) — none of these factors touch it. What WOULD touch it: an NH surface with a longer isotopic memory — a deeper annual-mean NH ML (the North Atlantic's winter mixing; the Holte climatology already drives the SEASONAL shape per hemisphere, mean-preserving on one `h_ml`), i.e. a per-hemisphere mean `h_ml`. Untested; the seasonal machinery makes it a contained change (a hemispheric factor on the mean inside `ccx_grid_at_h_ml`'s call).
**(B) A Southern Ocean column — the 3-column model.** NH, low-latitude, SO, each with its own surface and deep, the SO surface fed by a ~30-Sv Deacon cell from the global interior and returning at depth (AAIW / AABW), the interior shared. This is what §2's pre-check says is compatible with the deep age. It answers the SH half of gate D and the "why is the SO light" question physically; it does NOT answer the NH half (§3) and it changes the atmosphere's ocean coupling everywhere (every registered mode re-fitted; the app's box count; the pump field per column). Cost: weeks, a new spinup, a new port. Verdict: the physics is right and the target is wrong — build it for the SO science, not for gate D's asymmetry.
**(C) Accept gate D as a named red and the offset as a named red.** They pull opposite ways on the one lever that moves both; the model has no mechanism that moves both toward observation. Honest, cheap, and it keeps the two diagnostics alive for the next structure. This is the default if (A)'s NH-side test fails.
**Recommendation.** (A)'s cheap half first — TWO raw scans before any row or refit: a per-hemisphere mean `h_ml` (NH deeper; does NH ml D rise toward +1.28 and the residual move?), and `k_w_fac_SH` 1.14 WITH the isoflux tap (`diag_d13c_isoflux_budget.R`, 3.3 min) to see which process pays the −102. If the NH-ML test moves gate D and the residual the SAME way, (A) is worth the row and the refit; if not, (C), and (B) goes on the science list on its own merits.
