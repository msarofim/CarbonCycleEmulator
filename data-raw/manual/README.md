# Manually supplied raw inputs (TRACKED IN GIT — not reproducible by download)

Everything in `data-raw/downloads/` is regenerated by `data-raw/download_data.R`.
The files **here** are different: they were supplied by hand because
**the Scripps CO2 Program website (scrippsco2.ucsd.edu) returns HTTP 404 on every
asset path** as of 2026-08-09, so `download_data.R` cannot fetch them. They are
therefore committed to the repository — losing them means losing the data.

Supplied by Marcus Sarofim, 2026-08-09. Archive dates are stated inside each file
header (flask archives dated 19-Apr-2024 and 21-Feb-2025).

## Column trap

The Scripps monthly files have 10 columns. **Column 5 is the raw monthly value
(NaN where no measurement exists); columns 7–10 are FIT and GAP-FILLED series.**
Only column 5 is used as an observation — treating the filled columns as data would
fabricate observations and understate uncertainty. Coverage of real column-5 values:

- CO2: 711 of 816 monthly slots real; the 1950s decade is only 11 of 36.
- d13C: 528 of 576 monthly slots real.

## Why the MLO daily flask file is NOT used for monthly CO2

Mauna Loa flask sampling averages ~3.5 samples/month, and MLO has strong seasonal and
synoptic variability. Monthly means built from those flasks differ from the NOAA monthly
record with a scatter of sd 2.5-4.7 ppm, and the mean offset is not even sign-stable
(+0.239 ppm over all months, -0.218 ppm restricted to months with >=4 flasks). It is
therefore useless for detecting the ~0.1 ppm inter-network scale offset, and noisy as a
calibration target. South Pole is the opposite case -- so clean and stable that a
flask-based monthly product has sd 0.262 ppm against NOAA.

**MLO monthly CO2 needs the in-situ continuous record**, i.e. `monthly_merge_co2_mlo.csv`.

## Citation

H. A. Meijer (2001), *Exchanges of atmospheric CO2 and 13CO2 with the terrestrial
biosphere and oceans from 1978 to 2000. I. Global aspects*, SIO Reference Series
No. 01-06, Scripps Institution of Oceanography. Data: R. F. Keeling and S. J. Walker,
Scripps CO2 Program. d13C is on the Scripps isotope scale (Lueker et al. 2020);
CO2 is on the 2012 SIO manometric mole fraction scale.
