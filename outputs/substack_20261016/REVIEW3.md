## 1. Fix before posting

1. **The buffer paragraph still says the carbonate came from aragonite.** "But where did that carbonate come from?
   It was probably living its life as calcium carbonate aka aragonite, … the building blocks for shells and corals."
   The rest of the paragraph is now right, and this sentence contradicts it: the carbonate ion is already dissolved
   in seawater, and the uptake does not dissolve solid aragonite. The coral link is that shell-builders use the same
   dissolved carbonate ion to make aragonite, so the uptake leaves them less of it. Turning the question into that
   statement keeps the aragonite footnote and the corals sentence where they are.
2. Typos in the revised sentences: "an carbonate ion" → "a carbonate ion"; "carbonate ions gets used up" → "get used
   up"; "der Werf" → "van der Werf"; Figure X8 "responsae" → "response".
3. **The 2021 quote contains Schwartz's own slip**: "cessation of anthropogenic emissions atmospheric would result…"
   is verbatim from his abstract. Add "[sic]" after "atmospheric" so readers don't take it for yours. The comma in
   "was that, 'cessation" can go.
4. **Archer footnote**: Archer et al. (2009) has eleven authors, so "lead author of". The citation and link are right
   (Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences 37:117–134; Crossref).

## 3. Optional

1. **A sharper version of the Schwartz test.** The 60% in his quote is his reading of the models' pulse response
   (0.41 left at 100 years). The models agree with him on the pulse (36–38% left) and disagree on the excess (66–71%
   left against his 40%). That puts the error exactly where his "second order" sentence is. Related: "a third
   remaining from a pulse" is 36–38%, so "just over a third".
2. **A number for the 2021 paragraph.** At his own stop year (2017) and with his lifetime definition, CarboTope keeps
   64% of the excess after a century, an equivalent lifetime of 224 years against his 78–140 (REVIEW2 §2.6).
3. **Archer.** Archer and Brovkin (2008, Climatic Change 90; Crossref) is titled "The millennial atmospheric lifetime
   of anthropogenic CO2" and predates the 2009 review, if "changed the conversation" should point at the first paper.
   Schwartz's 2012 report quotes Archer et al. (2009) at length, and his 2021 reply to Archer says he adopted the
   qualitative definition of adjustment time "advanced by Archer et al. in their 2009 paper".
4. A figure option: mark Schwartz's assumed 40% at +100 years on Figure X8, so the reader sees his number against
   both curves. Not made; say if you want it.
